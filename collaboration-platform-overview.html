Platform overview

Context, attached where it belongs.

Comments, annotations, collections, tags, and follows as one shared service for the whole portfolio. People add context to objects other platforms own, without copying them, and that context carries the same access, provenance, and lifecycle discipline as the thing it sits on.

01

What this is

Every platform needs people to talk about its objects: a comment on a record, a note on a span of a translation, a pin on a map feature, a working set of sources gathered for a question. Built separately, each platform grows its own comments, its own folders, and its own tag list, none of which talk to each other, and a person’s context ends up scattered across a dozen systems. This platform is the one place human context lives, for every object in the portfolio.

Its central rule is that context is anchored, not copied. An annotation points at its target by stable identity and position: a Records record, an LPS segment, a GSP feature, a TESSERA entity, a span in a document. The home platform keeps owning the object; this platform owns what people said about it. A collection holds references, never duplicates, so nothing in it can drift away from the original.

Access is inherited, never widened. A comment on a compartmented document is itself compartmented. Context can be narrower than its target, never broader. Visibility is decided at read time from relationships and conditions in the IAM services, so a screened person never sees a thread about something they are not allowed to see, and revoking access to a target darkens everything attached to it at once.

Human context is not automated enrichment. Entity extraction, geocoding, and translation belong to TESSERA, GSP, and LPS. This platform holds what people add. Where SI helps, such as summarizing a long thread or suggesting tags, its output is labeled as machine-suggested and stored apart from what a person asserted, so nobody mistakes a suggestion for a colleague’s judgment.

And it is informal by default, governed when declared. A collection is a working set, not a record. When something here becomes evidence, such as a decision reached in a thread, it is declared into Records through Records’ own action endpoint, and from that moment Records’ retention and holds apply. Collaboration never quietly takes on records obligations, and nothing escapes them once declared.

02

What it does

The capability in plain terms, described by what people get rather than how it is built.

Comments and threads

Threaded discussion on any object in the portfolio, with replies, resolution, and reopening. Mentions reach the right people without showing them anything they cannot already see.

Annotations on a span

Mark a passage, a segment, an image region, or a map feature and say something about it. When the target changes, the anchor re-attaches or is flagged as orphaned rather than silently drifting.

Collections across platforms

Working sets that mix sources from LPS, features from GSP, records from Records, and entities from TESSERA. Every item is a reference, so the collection always shows the current object.

Tags that mean something

Free tags for personal use; controlled tags drawn from the shared reference data service for anything other people rely on, so one term means one thing across the portfolio.

Follow and notify

Watch an object, a thread, or a collection and get replies and changes in one feed. The feed is trimmed to what you may see at the moment it is read, not when it was sent.

Private by default

Bookmarks, personal notes, and drafts belong to one person and never appear in shared views, search results, or notifications for anyone else.

Sharing that cannot widen

Share a thread or collection with people or teams. A share decides who sees the context, and never grants access to the objects the context points at.

Revocation that reaches context

Lose access to a target and every comment, annotation, and collection entry about it goes dark everywhere at once, including in notifications and search.

Who said it, about what

Every comment records its author, time, and the exact version of the target it was written about, and whether a person or SI produced it.

Declare to Records

When a thread or collection becomes evidence, it is declared into Records as a record. Records’ retention and holds take over from that moment.

Search over context

Find the comment you half remember, or everything said about one entity across platforms, with results security-trimmed at query time.

Headless on purpose

Every capability is API and event stream. SuperApps, Donovan, the document editor, and the ATHENA drawer all consume the same surface with the same guarantees.

03

How far does a piece of context travel?

Every comment, annotation, and collection sits on one of four tiers of reach. Moving up a tier is a deliberate act by a person, never a side effect, and at every tier visibility is still bounded by access to the target.

PrivateOnly you
PERSONAL · NEVER INDEXED FOR OTHERS

Bookmarks, personal notes, drafts, and private tags. They exist for one person and never surface in anyone else’s view, search, or feed. Most context starts here.

SharedNamed people
THREADS · SHARED COLLECTIONS

Context shared with specific people or a team. Each reader sees it only where they can also see the target, so a share can narrow who sees context, never widen who sees the object.

PublishedOthers rely on it
CURATED · ATTRIBUTED · VERSIONED

Context a community depends on: pinned annotations, curated collections, controlled tags. It is attributed and versioned, edits keep their history, and its owner answers for it.

DeclaredHanded to Records
RECORD · RETENTION · HOLDS

Context that became evidence. It is declared into Records, fixed as a record, and governed by Records’ retention and holds from then on. The platform keeps a pointer to where it went.

Note Moving down is allowed only before declaration. A private note can be shared and later made private again; a declared record cannot be pulled back, because Records never rewinds history.

Visibility is an intersection, every time

Whether someone sees a piece of context is never decided by the share alone. It is the intersection of two answers: may this person see the target, and has the context been shared with them? Relationships come from OpenFGA, conditions from Cerbos, and the decision is made when the context is read, not when it was written. That is what keeps a thread from becoming a side door into a compartment, what makes revocation reach every comment immediately, and why a reader’s feed changes the moment their access does. The alternative, copying permissions onto each comment when it is written, is how collaboration tools leak.

04

How it works

Five views of the system, each with a plain-language read.

A

Three planes

The control plane holds the configuration that decides what may be annotated and how: the anchor types each platform supports, which vocabularies can tag what, notification rules, and retention defaults for informal content. It governs the data plane, where the context itself lives: annotations, threads, collections, tags, bookmarks, and shares. Every change emits into the activity plane, where notifications go out, search indexes update, declarations hand off to Records, and the event log accumulates.

B

Anchored, never copied

An annotation has two parts. The anchor says exactly what it is about: the target’s stable identity, the version that was annotated, and a selector for the position within it, such as a character span, an LPS segment, an image region, or a map feature. The body says what the person said. The home platform keeps the object; this platform keeps the anchor and the body, and stores the body in the Atheneum as content the organization created. Following the W3C Web Annotation model keeps anchors portable across every target type.

C

Who sees a piece of context

Two questions are asked every time context is read. May this person see the target, answered from relationships in OpenFGA such as matter teams, walls, and compartment membership. And was the context shared with them, at what tier. Cerbos combines the two with any conditions, such as purpose or time, and the answer is the intersection. If either half says no, the context is not shown, and its existence is not hinted at.

D

Revocation reaches context

When a home platform removes someone’s access to an object, the IAM services publish the change. Because visibility is computed at read time, every thread, annotation, and collection entry about that object goes dark for that person immediately, and the same event trims their notification feed and search results. Nothing has to be found and deleted, so nothing can be missed.

E

Where it sits

Every experience reaches context through one API: the SuperApps, Donovan, the document editor, and the ATHENA drawer. Underneath, the platform anchors into the domain platforms and TESSERA, asks the IAM services who may see what, stores bodies in the Atheneum, and draws controlled tags from the shared reference data service. It owns none of those things, which is what lets it serve all of them.

05

The API surface

Resource families grouped by plane. Everything below is REST plus a webhook-subscribable event stream; there is no other way in.

Control plane
Anchor types

/anchor-types

The target kinds each platform exposes and the selector schema for each: character spans, segments, image regions, map features, entities. A new platform registers here to become annotatable.

Vocabulary bindings

/vocabulary-bindings

Which controlled vocabularies from the reference data service may tag which anchor types. The vocabularies themselves live in reference data, not here.

Notification rules

/notification-rules

Defaults for what a follow delivers and how often, tuned per team, so the feed informs without flooding.

Retention

/retention-defaults

How long informal context is kept before purge, per tier. Declared context is out of scope here: Records governs it.

Data plane
Annotations

/annotations

W3C Web Annotation shaped: target, selector, body, motivation, author, and the target version it was written against.

Discussion

/threads · /comments

Threaded comments on any anchor, with replies, mentions, resolution, and reopening. Edits keep their history.

Working sets

/collections · /items

Ordered sets of references across platforms, with notes per item. Items resolve to the current target at read time.

Classification

/tags

Free tags for personal use and controlled tags bound to reference data vocabularies, applied to any anchor.

Personal

/bookmarks · /notes

Private pointers and notes that belong to one person and never appear in shared views, search, or feeds.

Reach

/shares

Who a thread or collection is shared with, at which tier. A share never grants access to the targets themselves.

Activity plane
Follow

/subscriptions · /notifications

Follows on objects, threads, and collections, delivered through one feed that is security-trimmed when read.

Audit

/events

The append-only log of every create, edit, share, tier change, and declaration, with webhook subscriptions for downstream systems.

Findability

/search

Full-text and structured search over context, trimmed against target access and shares per result at query time.

Evidence

/declarations

Hands a thread or collection to Records’ action endpoint to become a record, and keeps the pointer to where it went.

Integrity

/reanchoring

Re-attaches anchors when targets change, and reports orphans when a span or feature no longer exists.

Portability

/exports

Context exported in open Web Annotation format, so nothing people wrote is locked inside this platform.

07

Decided, and still open

The commitments the design already makes, and the questions the next design sessions need to close.

Locked in

  • Context anchors to its target by stable identity, version, and position, and never copies the target
  • The home platform owns the object; this platform owns what people said about it
  • Visibility is the intersection of target access and the share, decided when context is read
  • Context may be narrower than its target, never broader
  • Revoking access to a target darkens everything attached to it, immediately
  • Collections hold references, not copies; Records files stay separate, governed dossiers
  • Human assertions and SI suggestions are stored and shown as different things
  • Bodies are content the organization creates and live in the Atheneum
  • Controlled tags come from the shared reference data service, which this platform does not own
  • Declared context belongs to Records and cannot be pulled back
  • Headless: API and event stream only

Open questions

  • Build or reuse: Tiptap’s comments inside documents, a self-hosted annotation server for everything else, or one service for both
  • Whether to adopt the W3C Web Annotation model as is, or a constrained profile per target type
  • Which informal context a legal hold reaches, and when Records’ obligations begin to apply
  • Whether annotations feed THEMIS as calibration signals, and how to keep that from becoming surveillance of analysts
  • What happens to context when a target is merged, split, or superseded in its home platform
  • Notification defaults that inform without flooding
  • The platform’s name: “Collaboration” is a working title
