Subject: Naming the season ahead (and what comes first)

Team,

I recently listened to Josh Woodward, who leads Google Labs, talk about how his teams work. One realization of his stuck with me: teams do not operate at one steady speed all year, and pretending they do is how people get hurt. His answer is to name the season the team is in, out loud, so everyone knows what kind of effort is being asked of them and for how long. One of the seasons he names is explosive endurance.

The easiest way I know to explain it is through training. No serious athlete trains at the same intensity every week. Coaches plan the year in blocks. There are base-building blocks, where you put in steady, sustainable work. There are race-prep blocks, where intensity goes up, sessions get harder, and you ask more of your body than you normally would. And then there is the taper and recovery, where you deliberately pull back so the body can absorb the work and come back stronger. The athletes who get injured are usually the ones who never name the block they are in, so every week quietly becomes a race-prep week until something breaks.

Explosive endurance is the race-prep block. It is a defined stretch where we push harder than usual, with a clear start, a clear finish, and a recovery built in on the other side. Both words matter. Explosive means we bring real intensity. Endurance means we pace ourselves so we can sustain it and still be standing at the end.

So let me name our season. We are entering an explosive endurance period through [end date / milestone]. For us, that means:

- The work will ask more of us than usual for a while. I would rather say that plainly now than let it sneak up on anyone.
- It has an end. When we reach [milestone], we shift into recovery, and I will name that season too. Rest is part of the plan, not a reward we might get to.
- Family and self still come first. That does not pause because a deadline is close, and it matters even more with the holidays approaching. If you have holiday plans, keep them. Let your lead know early so we plan around you, not the other way around.
- Speak up early. If the load is becoming unsustainable, tell me or your lead. In training terms, that is the twinge you report before it becomes an injury. It is information I need, not a complaint, and it will never count against you.
- Look out for each other. If you see a teammate running on empty, check in and help share the load.

One more thing worth saying. Design is a wicked problem. There is no final answer, every solution reshapes the problem, and there will always be one more thing we could improve. Work like that will happily take every hour we give it. That is exactly why pace matters. A team that burns out in November cannot do its best thinking in February.

I am proud of how this team shows up, and I know we will deliver. I want us to come out the other side whole, with something left in the tank for what comes next.

Thank you for everything you do.

Build well,
Adam
